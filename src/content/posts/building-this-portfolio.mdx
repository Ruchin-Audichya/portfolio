## The brief I gave myself

Most developer portfolios fall into one of two camps. Either they're a static template that says "I exist", or they're a 3D demo so heavy on visuals that you can't actually find the projects. I wanted neither.

The brief was simple: prove I can build something visually demanding, and at the same time make the hiring information so clean that a recruiter who hates 3D websites still leaves with the resume.

## The stack

Nothing exotic.

- **Next.js 14 (App Router)** for the framework and the static-export-friendly routing.
- **Three.js (raw, no React Three Fiber)** for the hero scene, because the rendering is multi-pass and I wanted full control of the render targets.
- **GLSL** hand-written shaders for the noise prebake, the accretion disk, the gravitational-lensing distortion, and the post-processing composite.
- **Framer Motion** for scroll-linked section reveals.
- **Lenis** for smooth scroll on desktop, disabled on mobile and reduced-motion contexts.
- **TailwindCSS** for the design system.
- **MDX** for posts like this one.

## The hero scene, explained

The black hole isn't a model. It's a few primitives plus shaders.

- A **noise texture** is pre-baked once into a render target using classic 3D Perlin noise. The accretion disk samples this noise at four offsets to fake animated turbulence without recomputing it per frame.
- The **accretion disk** is an inverted cylinder geometry with a custom fragment shader that maps a vertical gradient texture against the noise-distorted UVs.
- A **starfield** is 8,000 GPU points (3,000 on mobile) with per-vertex sizes and HSL colors, drawn through a tiny shader that fakes a soft glow with a `1/distance` falloff.
- A **lensing pass** renders a simple "hole" billboard plus a horizontal disc into a separate distortion render target. That texture's red channel is sampled in post-processing to push pixels toward a convergence point — the projected origin of the black hole on screen — which is what gives the look of light bending around the singularity.
- The **post-processing composite** then samples the main scene, applies chromatic aberration scaled by the lensing distance, adds film grain, and vignettes the edges.

```glsl
// The core distortion line, simplified:
vec2 dir = uConvergence - vUv;
vec2 uvD = vUv + dir * dist * 0.3;

// then chromatic-aberration sampling:
float r = texture2D(uScene, uvD + vec2(cos( 0.0), sin( 0.0)) * caStr).r;
float g = texture2D(uScene, uvD + vec2(cos( 2.1), sin( 2.1)) * caStr).g;
float b = texture2D(uScene, uvD + vec2(cos(-2.1), sin(-2.1)) * caStr).b;
```

Two render passes per frame, one composite to the screen. Earlier versions did three passes (an extra direct-to-screen render) and burned GPU for nothing — that was cut as part of a perf pass.

## Performance tradeoffs I actually made

A 3D hero is an indulgence. It's worth nothing if it kills the page on a mid-range Android. So:

- **Star count is halved on mobile** (3,000 vs 8,000), antialias is off on mobile, and the device pixel ratio is clamped to 1.
- **The animation loop pauses on `visibilitychange`** so background tabs cost zero GPU.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` disables camera roll and drift.** The scene still renders so the page doesn't look broken, but the auto-rotation is gone.
- **All sections below the fold are wrapped in a custom `LazySection`** that uses `IntersectionObserver` with a 300px root margin. Heavy components only mount when they're about to be visible.
- **`next/dynamic` with `ssr: false`** for the WebGL component itself, so the initial HTML is small and Lighthouse metrics aren't pulled down by the canvas mount.

## The hiring layer on top of the visual layer

The 3D scene is the first impression. Everything below it is the actual hiring page.

- The **About section** is first-person prose with a sticky portrait on desktop. The bio walks from PC-building in 2017 to the work I'm proudest of today, with the resume numbers (169K records, 1.3M edges, +18 percentage points) embedded inside the story rather than as a list.
- The **Recruiter Snapshot** is a 30-second skim card: where I fit, how I work, proof points.
- The **Key Achievements strip** has six accent-colored cards covering hackathon standings, project metrics, and community reach.
- The **Credentials section** features Credly badges with brand-tinted glows and a Verified pill on each card. The whole card is the click target to Credly — no exposed URLs.
- Each **project case study** is its own page with its own SoftwareApplication and BreadcrumbList JSON-LD schema, plus a "View on GitHub" link to the source.

## SEO that actually targets the name

The bigger goal is that searching "Ruchin Audichya" surfaces this site, not LinkedIn alone.

- A visually-hidden `SeoIdentity` component at the top of the page acts as the canonical text identity for crawlers — name, role, location, paragraphs of name-dense copy, and inbound links to every public profile.
- The head emits five different JSON-LD blocks: `Person` with `alternateName`, `hasOccupation` with skills, awards, and credential URLs from Credly; `WebSite` with a `SearchAction`; `ProfilePage`; a six-question `FAQPage` for direct-name queries; plus per-page `BreadcrumbList`, `Article`, and `SoftwareApplication`.
- `rel="me"` links connect this domain to GitHub, LinkedIn, X, and Instagram so Google can confirm the same person owns those profiles.
- `robots.ts` explicitly allows Googlebot, Bingbot, GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, and Google-Extended. Being indexed by AI answer engines is a 2026 name-search signal too.
- `geo.region`, `geo.placename`, and ICBM coordinates point at Jaipur for local-pack signals.
